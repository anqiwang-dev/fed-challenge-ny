3NY Fed's Proposed Decision

Raise by 25 basis points to 4.00%–4.25%

  • Inflation is still above target and core inflation momentum is sticky.
  • Q3 GDP and domestic demand show the economy can likely absorb one more small step.
  • A marginal increase helps prevent premature easing of financial conditions and reinforces inflation credibility.

What would make us pause instead?

  • Clear evidence of softer demand.
  • A broader labor-market deterioration.
  • Faster disinflation in core measures.

Source: Federal Reserve October 28 FOMC; BEA Q3 GDP and September PCE; BLS October CPI. 25 bp hike is a proposed classroom position, not official Fed policy. Target range: FRED DFEDTARL, DFEDTARU.

Growth is supported by consumption and investment; strong domestic demand limits the case for immediate easing.

5State of the U.S. Labor Market: Maintaining a “Low-Hire, Low-Fire” Equilibrium

Unemployment holding steady around 4%

  • Layoffs are low.
  • Hiring and job-switching are subdued.
  • Average hourly earnings are up 3.1% over the year, roughly in line with core PCE inflation.
  • Wall Street: securities employment hit a record 207,400 in 2025 and is on pace for about 5,300 more in 2026.
  • First-half pretax profits were $45.9 billion, up 51.3%: profits and pay are booming, with only modest job growth.
  • NYC private employment is down 16,300 so far this year; excluding health care, it is down 32,000 over 12 months.

Source: BLS (bls.gov; FRED UNRATE). New York: NY State Comptroller, NYC securities industry report 2026 (osc.ny.gov); roic.ai, Oct 6 2026.

6State of the U.S. Labor Market: Cooling Labor Force Participation Rate

Participation rate shifting down

  • Population shifts (aging labor force) put downward pressure on participation without affecting the unemployment rate.
  • Involuntary part-time work jumped by 339,000 to 4.8 million · long-term unemployed make up 27.6% of the jobless.

Source: BLS (bls.gov; FRED CIVPART).

8Inflation: Implications for Our Decision

A single marginal increase is a risk-management move—not the start of a new hiking cycle.

Evidence supporting a 25 bp hike

  • Inflation is still above target.
  • Domestic demand remains resilient.
  • Two FOMC members already preferred hiking in October.

Why not hold instead?

  • Policy works with lags.
  • Some inflation is supply-driven and not solved by rates.
  • September real PCE was roughly flat.

Source: BEA core PCE price index (FRED PCEPILFE) and BLS core CPI (FRED CPILFESL), through September and October 2026; BEA Q3 GDP, September PCE; Federal Reserve October 28 FOMC statement; New York Fed SCE November 9.

9Closing Recommendation

Raise by 25 bp to 4.00%–4.25%

  • Inflation remains above target and broad enough to warrant additional restraint.
  • GDP and domestic demand remain resilient.
  • A marginal move preserves anti-inflation credibility without signalling an aggressive path.

Methods

  • Chart series come from FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, retrieved November 24, 2026.
  • Year over year is the 12-month percent change in a price index; month over month is the 1-month change.
  • A 2% annual rate works out to about 0.165% a month: 1.021/12 − 1.
  • GDP growth and contributions are seasonally adjusted annual rates from BEA; contributions add up to growth, up to rounding.
  • October 2025 household-survey and CPI data were not collected during the federal government shutdown. The lines bridge that month; hovering it shows it was not collected.
  • On the rate charts, December's step is our proposal (or the pause alternative), placed at the December meeting.
